# Computational report: de novo PD-L1 miniprotein binders

## 1. Problem

Describe the PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint interface and why a compact alternative binder is worth exploring. Avoid calling PD-L1 undruggable; the pathway is clinically validated and already targeted by antibodies.

## 2. Hypothesis

A de novo miniprotein that binds the PD-1-facing surface of PD-L1 may be a computationally plausible blocking candidate.

## 3. Methods

- Target preparation and chain mapping
- Interface/contact definition
- RFdiffusion backbone generation
- ProteinMPNN sequence design
- ColabFold/AlphaFold2-Multimer validation
- Structural overlap and ranking criteria

Record exact versions, seeds, number of attempted designs, and filtering thresholds.

## 4. Results

Populate from `results/` only. Report attempted designs, successful designs, failed predictions, filter counts, score distributions, and sequence diversity.

## 5. Figures

Add the target interface and best computational finalist overlay after real runs are complete.

## 6. Limitations

Computational confidence is not affinity or functional blockade. No experimental validation, expression testing, serum-stability testing, immunogenicity assessment, or cellular assay is included unless separately documented.

## 7. Next steps

Prioritize synthesis and biophysical testing only after selecting candidates with reproducible structure consistency, target-interface overlap, sequence diversity, and acceptable developability properties.
